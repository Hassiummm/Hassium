General Info

In advanced mathematics, students are expected to develop a certain level of mathematical maturity in order to understand and work with abstract concepts. Although this notion is widely recognized, there is no precise way to measure such maturity, nor a definitive method for teaching the writing of mathematical proofs. These notes are intended to serve as a transition from elementary mathematics to proof-based formal mathematics.

Contents

(1) Basic logic which forms the language of mathematical proofs.
(2) Some Axioms in ZFC, examples of sets, and finite induction.
(3) More Axioms in ZFC, examples of functions, and canonical decomposition of functions.
(4) The construction of \(\mathbb{Q}\) and the Cantor-Schröder-Bernstein theorem.
(5) Vector Spaces, linear maps, and several examples.
(6) Groups, the first isomorphism theorem, and group presentations.
(7): Rings?
(8): Sequences, convergence, and the construction of \(\mathbb{R}\).
(9): Topological spaces, several topological invariants, and Tikhonov's theorem.
(10): Categories, functors, and Yoneda lemma based on the categories discussed in previous sections.
